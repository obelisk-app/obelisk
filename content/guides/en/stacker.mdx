## The game

Blocks fall. You fit them into rows. A completed row clears — and instead of scoring you points, it drops **garbage lines** into an opponent's well, with a single hole punched through them for them to dig out. Last player standing wins. One to six players; the solo table is a perfectly good way to practise.

<Shot name="games/stacker-well" caption="A well mid-match, with the ghost of the falling piece showing where it lands." maxWidth={340} />

Everyone in the match starts from the **same seed**, so everyone gets the same pieces in the same order. A match is a race, not a lottery. The seed comes from the table options, or from the table id itself — a hash, so it is unpredictable before the table exists and identical for everybody after.

## Why it can't work the way the other games do

Chain Reaction and Vesta are turn-based: one player moves, everybody replays the move, the board advances. Stacker has no seat "to move" — six boards are running at once — and a relay round-trip per keypress would feel like playing through treacle.

So it splits in half:

| | Where it runs |
|---|---|
| Board, gravity, input, the 60 Hz loop | **Your machine**, locally |
| Attacks, top-outs, checkpoints | **The relay**, as kind 2390 events |

Your well never leaves your computer. What leaves is what happened because of it.

<Shot name="games/stacker-table" caption="A three-way match. Your well at full speed; opponents as miniature boards updated from their checkpoints, with attack and line counts." maxWidth={620} />

Opponents are drawn coarsely on purpose. Their real boards are running on their own machines and only reach you every few seconds, so painting a detailed board would be painting a lie. What you get instead is the thing that stays true between updates: how buried they are.

## The three events

| op | content | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `attack` | `{seat, target, lines, hole, nonce}` | garbage from one seat to another |
| `topout` | `{seat}` | that player is out |
| `checkpoint` | `{seat, frame, attacksSent, linesCleared, stackHeight, inputs, board}` | progress, plus the input log that produced it |

Attacks are coalesced before they go out: three quick clears become one event with the lines summed. Signing an event takes milliseconds with a local key and considerably longer with a browser extension, so nothing is ever signed between two frames — outbound traffic leaves on its own timer, about four times a second.

## How it stays honest

The engine is a pure function of `(seed, inputs)`. That is what makes the checkpoint interesting: it carries the sender's compressed **input log**, so anybody can replay it against the shared seed, rebuild their board, and check whether the attacks they claimed to send actually happened. A seat whose claims its own log does not support gets marked in the UI.

<Callout type="warn" title="This detects cheating, it does not prevent it">
Garbage lands before the checkpoint justifying it arrives, so a modified client
can land one round of unearned attacks before it is flagged. Preventing that
outright means lockstep — a relay round-trip per frame — which would destroy
the thing the game is for. The trade is deliberate. It also catches invented
attacks, not a human being helped by a bot that plays legitimately well.
</Callout>

Incoming garbage is fed through the same input log as everything else, so a replay reproduces the board that actually happened, garbage included.

## Feel

The numbers that decide whether a game like this plays well:

- **DAS 10 frames, ARR 2** — how long a held key waits before repeating, and how fast it repeats after.
- **Gravity from 48 frames per step down to 4**, on the classic curve: gentle at the start, sharply faster through the teens, then a hard floor so it stays humanly playable. Ten lines is a level.
- **Lock delay 30 frames with 15 slide resets** — the moment after a piece lands where you can still slide it.
- The board draws straight to a canvas from the game loop. React never sees a frame; it only re-renders for the numbers a human reads, about eight times a second.

Controls are rebindable from the table (⌨ **controls**), and the binding is stored per browser — it is a device preference, not account state, so it never goes near the relay.

## Sound

Effects are synthesized at runtime: line clears pitched by how many lines went, a distinct chime for spins, a rumble for garbage, a falling tone for a top-out. No audio files, and they fire straight off engine events.

The music is the royalty-free set from [TETRA](https://github.com/soyezequiel/tetris-para-luna-negra) by **soyezequiel**, a La Crypta hackathon project, credited in the game UI with a link back. It shuffles once per session and advances on its own — there is no picker. If a track cannot load, or the browser refuses to autoplay it, a generative synth bed plays instead, so the game is never silent because of a missing file.

## Playing it

`/play` in a channel, pick Stacker, set a seed if you want a specific board order, and start. Because every player runs their own board, an account holds exactly **one** seat: hot-seat is meaningless when everybody is playing at the same time.
